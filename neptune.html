NEPTUNE: IPv6 Multihoming Architecture for Multi-ISP Networks

2024 • IPv6, Multihoming, DHCPv6-PD, NPTv6, Protocol Design

Summary

Most fixed Internet access networks still rely on IPv4, typically assigning a single public address to the customer router and using NAT to provide connectivity to internal hosts. While effective as a workaround for address exhaustion, this model was never part of the original Internet design and introduces limitations for end-to-end services such as point-to-point VPNs.

IPv6 addresses the public addressing problem through a vastly larger address space and native auto-configuration mechanisms. However, IPv4 and IPv6 are not interoperable, requiring transition mechanisms and increasing operational complexity, especially in mixed deployments.

A particularly unresolved issue is IPv6 multihoming in residential and small enterprise networks. Unlike IPv4 NAT-based setups, IPv6 requires hosts to be addressed from each ISP prefix, complicating traffic engineering, load balancing and failure recovery. NEPTUNE proposes a unified architecture to address these limitations through coordinated prefix translation, traffic steering and policy control.

Motivation

The continuous growth of Internet-connected devices is increasingly exposing the architectural limitations of IPv4-based networks. The lack of public addressing per device complicates or prevents the deployment of services that rely on stable end-to-end connectivity.

At the same time, IPv6 lacks a practical solution for a very common real-world scenario: using multiple Internet connections simultaneously in residential or small enterprise environments. NEPTUNE is motivated by this gap, addressing an existing and operationally relevant problem rather than a speculative future requirement.

State of the Art

The closest reference to the problem addressed by NEPTUNE is found in section 3.2.4 (Multihoming) of RFC 7368 – IPv6 Home Networking Architecture Principles. While it acknowledges the complexity of multihoming and encourages minimising architectural overhead, it does not provide a complete framework suitable for real multi-ISP residential deployments.

However, the general multihoming problem is broad, and solutions suggested to date within the IETF have included complex architectures for monitoring connectivity, traffic engineering, identifier-locator separation, and connection survivability across multihoming events. (RFC 7368, 2014)

NEPTUNE builds on existing IPv6 and transition mechanisms rather than introducing entirely new ones, combining them into a coherent and operationally manageable architecture.

IPv6-Specific Protocols

  • DHCPv6-PD (RFC 8415): used by ISPs to delegate one or more IPv6 prefixes to the customer edge router, forming the basis of address assignment in residential IPv6 deployments.
  • Router Advertisements (RFC 4861): ICMPv6 messages used by routers to announce network prefixes and configuration parameters to hosts.
  • SLAAC (RFC 4862): allows hosts to autonomously generate IPv6 addresses within the advertised prefix, including Duplicate Address Detection to ensure uniqueness.
  • NPTv6 (RFC 6296): provides stateless translation between IPv6 prefixes. Although rarely deployed in practice, it is a key building block in NEPTUNE’s design.

IPv6 Transition Mechanisms

  • Dual Stack (RFC 4213): parallel IPv4 and IPv6 operation, offering full compatibility at the cost of duplicated addressing and management.
  • DS-Lite (RFC 6333): IPv6-only ISP networks with IPv4-only customer LANs, relying on NAT mechanisms and offering no native IPv6 connectivity to hosts.
  • 464XLAT (RFC 6877): commonly used in mobile networks, providing native IPv6 to hosts while enabling access to IPv4-only services through translation.

Aim of the Project

  1. Analyse the limitations of existing IPv6 protocols in multi-ISP residential deployments.
  2. Enable a seamless transition from IPv4 to IPv6 while preserving end-to-end communication in mixed environments.
  3. Propose NEPTUNE as a unified framework to simplify traditionally complex IPv6 multihoming configurations.

Preliminary Proposal

NEPTUNE is currently under development. The proposed architecture combines prefix translation, DNS-aware traffic handling and policy-driven load balancing, as illustrated in the diagram below.

Internally, hosts are configured with a preferred public IPv6 prefix advertised via Router Advertisements. When multiple ISP prefixes are available, only one is exposed to the LAN, while the Prefix Engine maintains a 1:1 NPTv6 mapping between external prefixes.

All outgoing traffic is routed through the NEPTUNE Virtual Interface, where flows are classified based on destination capabilities, circuit health and configured policies. Translation and encapsulation are handled transparently by dedicated data-plane modules.

Main Scenarios

  • IPv6 destinations: traffic is distributed across IPv6-capable circuits, with flow stickiness preserved to maintain return-path symmetry.
  • IPv4 destinations: public IPv4 circuits are prioritised when available; otherwise traffic is balanced across IPv4-capable links.
  • Failure handling: prefix translation and circuit selection adapt dynamically, without requiring internal network reconfiguration.
